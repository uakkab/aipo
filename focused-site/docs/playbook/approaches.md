# Transformation Approaches

No single approach works for every organization or use case. Use this page to understand the mix; you will apply it during Implementation.

## Approach Comparison

| Approach | What It Is | Best For | Main Strength | Main Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Framework-driven | Use defined standards and operating rules from day one | Regulated and process-mature environments | Predictable and scalable | Can feel slow in discovery phases |
| Hierarchical (top-down) | Leadership-led prioritization and mandate | Enterprise-wide alignment programs | Strong direction and accountability | Can miss frontline reality |
| Pragmatic (bottom-up) | Use-case-first experimentation and iteration | Fast-moving teams and emerging needs | Fast learning and rapid value proof | Fragmentation if not coordinated |
| Governance and trust | Risk-tiered controls across lifecycle | High-impact or sensitive deployments | Trust, safety, and auditability | Excess control can slow innovation |

## When To Use Each

- Use Framework-driven when requirements and controls are clear.
- Use Hierarchical when coordination across many teams is the bottleneck.
- Use Pragmatic when uncertainty is high and learning speed is critical.
- Use Governance and trust for all approaches, with deeper controls for higher risk tiers.

## Recommended Blend

1. Start pragmatic to discover high-value opportunities.
2. Apply hierarchical alignment to prioritize and fund.
3. Standardize with framework-driven delivery for scale.
4. Keep governance controls active through all stages.

## Tailoring Guidance

| Context | Delivery Bias |
| --- | --- |
| High uncertainty, low compliance burden | Adaptive and pragmatic |
| Moderate uncertainty, multi-team dependencies | Hybrid with hierarchical coordination |
| Low uncertainty, high compliance burden | Predictive and framework-driven |

Reference alignment: PMBOK tailoring, ITIL service strategy, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001.
